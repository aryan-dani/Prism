# MERIDIAN FIXTURES INC. — HUMAN RESOURCES POLICY MANUAL

**Document owner:** Human Resources Department
**Applies to:** All full-time and probationary employees of Meridian Fixtures Inc. (manufacturing, corporate, and regional office locations)
**Version:** 1.0 (Synthetic — Prism Project)

## 1. Introduction and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This manual sets out the policies and procedures governing the employment relationship between Meridian Fixtures Inc. ("the Company") and its employees. It is the single reference point for leave entitlements, reimbursement processes, standards of conduct, remote-work eligibility, grievance handling, disciplinary procedure, and separation from the Company.

### 1.2 Applicability
This manual applies to all confirmed and probationary employees across the Company's manufacturing plants, distribution centers, and corporate offices. Contract workers and third-party vendor staff are governed by their respective contractual agreements, not this manual, except where explicitly stated.

### 1.3 Employee Grades (referenced throughout this manual)
- **Grade W** — Plant/production workforce
- **Grade E1–E2** — Associates and Senior Associates (individual contributors)
- **Grade E3–E4** — Specialists and Senior Specialists (individual contributors)
- **Grade M1–M2** — Managers and Senior Managers
- **Grade M3+** — Department Heads, Directors, and above

### 1.4 Probation Period
All new hires (Grades E1 and above) serve a probation period of **6 months** from the date of joining. Probation may be extended once, by up to **3 months**, at the discretion of the Reporting Manager and HR Business Partner, if performance has not yet met confirmation criteria. Plant workforce (Grade W) probation is **3 months**, non-extendable.

## 2. Leave Policy

### 2.1 Leave Year
The leave year runs from **April 1 to March 31**, aligned with the Company's fiscal year. All accrual, carry-forward, and encashment rules below are computed on this cycle.

### 2.2 Casual Leave (CL)
- Entitlement: **12 days per leave year**, credited monthly in advance at **1 day per completed month**.
- Minimum unit: 0.5 day (half-day CL permitted).
- Maximum consecutive CL: **3 days**. Requests for more than 3 consecutive CL days require Reporting Manager approval and are adjusted against Earned Leave.
- Carry-forward: up to **5 unused CL days** may be carried into the next leave year. Any balance above 5 lapses on March 31.
- Encashment: CL is **not encashable** at any time, including at separation.

### 2.3 Sick Leave (SL)
- Entitlement: **10 days per leave year**, credited in full at the start of the leave year (April 1), pro-rated for new joiners based on remaining months in the current cycle.
- Medical certificate: mandatory for any SL request exceeding **2 consecutive days**.
- Carry-forward: up to **5 unused SL days** may be carried into the next leave year; balance above 5 lapses on March 31.
- Encashment: SL is not encashable during active employment. At separation, **50% of the unused SL balance (capped at 10 days)** is paid out as part of full-and-final settlement, at the employee's last-drawn basic daily rate.

### 2.4 Earned Leave / Privilege Leave (EL)
- Entitlement: **18 days per leave year**, accrued monthly at **1.5 days per completed month**.
- Advance notice: EL requests require a minimum of **3 working days'** advance notice, except for medical emergencies.
- Maximum accumulation: EL balance is capped at **45 days**. Accrual beyond 45 days is forfeited unless the employee encashes the excess (see below).
- Blackout period: EL requests during the **November 15 – December 31** peak production window require Department Head (Grade M3+) approval in addition to the Reporting Manager's.
- Encashment: employees may encash up to **10 EL days per leave year** while in active service, and up to **30 EL days** of the accumulated balance at the time of separation.

### 2.5 Maternity and Paternity Leave
- Maternity Leave: **26 weeks** of paid leave for the first two children (statutory entitlement), **12 weeks** for the third child onward, in line with applicable law.
- Paternity Leave: **10 working days**, to be availed within 3 months of the child's birth or adoption.
- Adoption Leave: **12 weeks** paid leave for the primary adoptive parent of a child under 1 year of age.

### 2.6 Bereavement Leave
**5 working days** of paid leave in the event of the death of an immediate family member (spouse, child, parent, sibling, or parent-in-law).

### 2.7 Compensatory Off
Employees required to work on a declared holiday or weekly off are entitled to a compensatory day off, to be availed within **30 calendar days** of the date worked, subject to Reporting Manager approval. Compensatory off cannot be carried forward past 30 days or encashed.

### 2.8 Leave Without Pay (LWP)
LWP may be granted at the Reporting Manager's and HR's discretion once all paid leave balances (CL, SL, EL) are exhausted. LWP exceeding **15 consecutive days** requires Department Head approval and is noted in the employee's service record.

## 3. Reimbursement Process (Policy and Workflow)

> **Note:** This section governs the *process, eligibility categories, and documentation* for employee reimbursements. Specific monetary **limits and approval-value thresholds** are defined in the Finance Policy Manual (Section 2, "Expense Reimbursement Limits") and are not duplicated here to avoid the two documents drifting out of sync — always refer to the Finance Policy Manual for current ₹ limits.

### 3.1 Eligible Reimbursement Categories
- Domestic and international business travel (airfare, rail, local conveyance, lodging, meals)
- Client entertainment and business development expenses
- Mobile phone and home internet allowance for eligible grades
- Relocation expenses for Company-initiated transfers
- Professional certification and training fees pre-approved by the Reporting Manager
- One-time work-from-home equipment setup (desk, chair, monitor) for approved WFH employees (see Section 5)

### 3.2 Submission Process
1. Employee submits an expense claim through the HRMS Expense Portal within **30 calendar days** of incurring the expense, attaching original receipts/invoices.
2. Claims submitted after 30 days require a written justification and Department Head approval to be processed.
3. The Reporting Manager reviews and approves/rejects the claim within **5 working days**.
4. Approved claims are routed to Finance for the sign-off levels specified in the Finance Policy Manual, based on claim amount.
5. Reimbursement is credited to the employee's registered bank account within **10 working days** of final Finance approval.

### 3.3 Documentation Requirements
All claims above the "no-receipt" threshold defined in the Finance Policy Manual must be accompanied by an original itemized receipt. Claims lacking required documentation are returned to the employee for correction, not rejected outright, unless resubmitted past the 30-day window without justification.

## 4. Code of Conduct and Ethics

### 4.1 Core Principles
Employees are expected to act with integrity, honesty, and respect in all professional dealings, in accordance with the Company's core values of design excellence, water conservation, and operational efficiency.

### 4.2 Conflict of Interest
Employees must disclose, in writing to HR, any outside financial interest, secondary employment, or family relationship that could reasonably be seen to conflict with Company interests. Non-disclosure of a material conflict of interest is treated as a **major misconduct** (see Section 7.3).

### 4.3 Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption
No employee may offer, give, solicit, or accept any bribe, kickback, or improper payment in connection with Company business. Gifts from vendors/customers valued above **₹2,500** must be declined or, if declining would be culturally inappropriate, disclosed to and surrendered to HR.

### 4.4 Confidentiality and Data Protection
Employees must protect confidential Company information, trade secrets, and customer/employee personal data, both during and after employment, consistent with the Company's data protection obligations. Unauthorized disclosure is grounds for disciplinary action up to and including termination.

### 4.5 Anti-Harassment and POSH Compliance
The Company maintains zero tolerance for workplace harassment, including sexual harassment, in compliance with the Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Act, 2013. All complaints are handled by the Internal Committee (IC) as described in Section 6.4. Retaliation against a complainant or witness is itself a major misconduct.

### 4.6 Use of Company Assets and IT Resources
Company equipment, email, and network access are provided for business use. Incidental personal use is permitted provided it does not interfere with work, violate law, or expose the Company to security risk. Use of Company IT resources to access, store, or distribute unlawful, discriminatory, or harassing content is strictly prohibited.

### 4.7 Workplace Safety
All employees, particularly plant workforce (Grade W), must comply with posted safety protocols, use provided personal protective equipment (PPE), and report unsafe conditions to their supervisor or the Safety Officer immediately. Willful safety-protocol violations that endanger self or others are treated as major misconduct.

### 4.8 Social Media and External Communication
Employees may not speak on behalf of the Company on social or public media without authorization from Corporate Communications. Personal social media use must not disclose confidential Company information or disparage the Company, colleagues, or customers.

## 5. Work From Home (WFH) / Hybrid Work Policy

### 5.1 Eligibility
WFH is available to confirmed employees, Grade E2 and above, in roles that do not require physical presence on the plant floor or at a customer-facing service location. Grade W (plant workforce) and customer-facing retail/service roles are not eligible.

### 5.2 WFH Allowance
Eligible employees may work from home up to **2 days per week**, subject to Reporting Manager approval and team coverage needs. Fully remote arrangements beyond 2 days/week require Department Head (Grade M3+) approval and are reviewed quarterly.

### 5.3 Core Collaboration Hours
Regardless of work location, all employees are expected to be reachable and available for meetings between **10:00 AM and 4:00 PM** on working days ("core hours").

### 5.4 Equipment and Security
The Company provides a one-time WFH equipment allowance (amount specified in the Finance Policy Manual). Employees must use Company-approved VPN and endpoint security software when accessing Company systems remotely, and must not allow non-employees to use Company equipment.

### 5.5 Performance Expectations
WFH is a privilege tied to sustained performance. Two consecutive quarters of below-expectation performance ratings result in WFH eligibility review by the Reporting Manager and HR.

## 6. Grievance Redressal Mechanism

### 6.1 Guiding Principle
Employees have the right to raise concerns about their employment without fear of retaliation. Grievances are handled promptly, confidentially, and fairly.

### 6.2 Three-Step Escalation Process
1. **Step 1 — Reporting Manager:** Employee raises the grievance verbally or in writing to their Reporting Manager, who must respond within **5 working days**.
2. **Step 2 — HR Business Partner (HRBP):** If unresolved or the grievance concerns the Reporting Manager directly, the employee escalates to the HRBP, who must investigate and respond within **7 working days**.
3. **Step 3 — Grievance Redressal Committee (GRC):** If still unresolved, the matter is referred to the GRC (comprising HR Head, an employee representative, and a Department Head not connected to the case), which issues a final decision within **15 working days** of referral.

### 6.3 Formal Grievance Register
All Step 2 and Step 3 grievances are logged in the confidential HR Grievance Register with outcome and closure date, retained for a minimum of 3 years.

### 6.4 POSH Complaints (Special Track)
Complaints of sexual harassment bypass the standard 3-step process and go directly to the Internal Committee (IC), which follows the statutory POSH Act, 2013 timeline: inquiry completed within **90 days** of the complaint, with interim relief (e.g., leave, reporting-line change) available to the complainant during the inquiry on request.

## 7. Disciplinary Procedure

### 7.1 Principle of Progressive Discipline
Except for major misconduct (Section 7.3), the Company follows a progressive discipline model, allowing employees the opportunity to correct behavior before separation is considered.

### 7.2 Minor Misconduct — Escalation Ladder
Applies to matters such as repeated tardiness, missed deadlines without cause, or minor policy non-compliance, within a rolling **12-month window**:
1. **Verbal Warning** (documented in writing to the employee's file)
2. **Written Warning** (if a second instance occurs)
3. **Final Written Warning + Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)** of **30–60 days** (third instance)
4. **Termination of Employment** (if the PIP is not successfully completed, or a fourth instance occurs within the window)

### 7.3 Major Misconduct — Immediate Action
Theft, fraud, workplace violence, sexual harassment, safety-protocol violation endangering life, breach of confidentiality causing material harm, or conviction of a criminal offense related to employment are treated as major misconduct:
- The employee may be placed on **suspension pending inquiry** (with subsistence allowance per applicable law) immediately upon credible allegation.
- An inquiry committee is constituted within **7 working days**.
- The inquiry is completed and a decision issued within **30 working days** of constitution, absent extraordinary circumstances.
- Outcomes range from written warning to **termination without notice**, depending on inquiry findings.

### 7.4 Right to Representation
An employee facing a disciplinary inquiry may be accompanied by one colleague of their choice during any inquiry hearing.

## 8. Notice Period and Resignation

### 8.1 Notice Period by Grade
| Employee Status / Grade | Notice Period |
|---|---|
| Probationary employee (any grade) | 15 days |
| Confirmed employee, Grade E1–E4 | 30 days |
| Confirmed employee, Grade M1 and above | 60 days |

### 8.2 Resignation Process
1. Employee submits a written resignation to their Reporting Manager and HR via the HRMS.
2. HR acknowledges receipt within **2 working days** and confirms the Last Working Day (LWD) based on the applicable notice period.
3. Buyout of notice period (paying the Company in lieu of serving notice, or vice versa) is permitted at the sole discretion of the Reporting Manager and HR, based on **the employee's last-drawn basic salary for the unserved/waived portion**.

### 8.3 Exit Formalities
- Knowledge transfer plan submitted and signed off by the Reporting Manager before LWD.
- Return of all Company assets (laptop, ID card, access cards, mobile device) on or before LWD.
- Exit interview conducted by HR.
- Full-and-final settlement (including EL/SL encashment per Sections 2.3–2.4) processed within **45 days** of LWD.

### 8.4 Absconding
An employee who is absent without approved leave or communication for **10 consecutive working days** is presumed to have abandoned employment; the Company may initiate separation proceedings without notice pay, following a documented attempt to contact the employee.

## 9. Attendance and Working Hours

### 9.1 Standard Working Hours
Corporate/office roles: **9:00 AM – 6:00 PM**, Monday–Friday, with a 1-hour lunch break. Plant workforce follows shift schedules posted at each facility (typically 8-hour shifts across 2–3 shifts/day).

### 9.2 Late Arrival
Arrival more than **15 minutes** after the shift/office start time on more than **3 occasions** in a calendar month is recorded as an attendance irregularity and may trigger the minor-misconduct escalation ladder (Section 7.2).

### 9.3 Overtime
Grade W and Grade E1–E2 employees required to work beyond standard hours are eligible for overtime compensation per the Finance Policy Manual's overtime pay-rate schedule. Grade M1 and above are not eligible for overtime pay and are compensated via the annual performance bonus structure instead.

## 10. Performance Appraisal (Summary)

Performance is reviewed on an **annual cycle** (April–March), with a **mid-year check-in** in October. Ratings use a 5-point scale (Outstanding, Exceeds Expectations, Meets Expectations, Needs Improvement, Unsatisfactory). Two consecutive "Needs Improvement" or one "Unsatisfactory" rating triggers a mandatory Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) of 60 days, run jointly by the Reporting Manager and HRBP.

---

## 11. Employee Referral Policy (Summary)

Current employees may refer external candidates for open positions via the HRMS Referral Portal. A referral bonus (amount specified in the Finance Policy Manual) is paid after the referred candidate completes **90 days** of continuous employment. Employees in Grade M2 and above may not refer candidates into roles they would directly manage, to avoid conflicts of interest.

---

## 12. Benefits and Perks (Summary)

All confirmed employees are enrolled in the Company's group health insurance (employee + immediate family), Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), and gratuity scheme per applicable statutory requirements. Grade M1 and above additionally receive an annual health check-up benefit. Specific coverage amounts and premium contributions are documented in the annual Benefits Guide issued separately by HR.
